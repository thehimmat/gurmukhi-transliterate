# Snap Gurbani to canon, rank everything else

> Research for [#16](https://github.com/thehimmat/gurmukhi-transliterate/issues/16), October 2026. Per-topic source notes are in [`notes/`](notes/); the scripts behind the measured figures are in [`scripts/`](scripts/) (run from any directory; `proto_match.py` needs the `@shabados/database` SQLite file).

For issue #16, the answer depends on whether the input is Gurbani. If a romanized line is Gurbani, it should be matched to a canonical verse rather than reversed word by word. A pure-Python prototype built on the public-domain Shabad OS corpus found the right SGGS line **97.7% of the time on clean input**. Everything else (single words, modern Punjabi, names) needs a lexicon-ranked reverse transliterator: candidate spellings come from the repo's own `SystemMap`s, inverted into a lattice, and are ranked by a Gurbani word-frequency list that compresses to about **311 KB gzipped**. Two pieces have to come first. A gated log-likelihood detector, with a neutral class for Sikh names and the option to abstain, has to separate English from romanized Gurmukhi. And `identify_system` has to be replaced with a likelihood scorer that reports equivalence sets. Today it uniquely identifies the right system on only **15% of single words**, and it scores an English pangram 0.756.

Three findings shape the plan. First, the systems people actually meet online are mostly not the thirteen maps in the repo. They are a few ASCII schemes generated by code: current BaniDB/STTM, legacy STTM, and Shabad OS, plus informal and 3HO spellings. Second, the repo's forward engine itself loses addak and nasals that the schemes would keep, so those engine bugs have to be fixed before any reverse is tested against engine output. Third, only Shabad OS can legally be bundled. BaniDB data comes with terms of service that require attribution, its logo and use "in its entirety". Neural models are not worth a hard dependency: IndicXlit, the best open model, gets only **40–47% top-1 on Punjabi** and needs about 1 GB of downloads.

## Five ASCII families account for most real romanized Gurbani

The romanizations found online come mostly from a few programs, not from people typing scholarly schemes. The BaniDB v2 API returns `transliteration.english` for every verse, with no diacritics. Retroflexes are marked with capitals and nasals with parentheses: `kiv sachiaaraa hoieeaai kiv kooRai tuTai paal ||` and `saibha(n)` ([BaniDB /v2/angs/2/G](https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/2/G)). The Khalis Foundation's MIT-licensed `anvaad-js` 1.5.1 reproduces those fields **byte for byte** on every line tested, so its code is in practice the specification for the scheme. It also shows behaviour that never appears in the repo's engine: addak written as an apostrophe (`si'khee`), ਢ→`dd` (the same as ਡ), and ਖ਼→`kh`, ਫ਼→`f` ([anvaad-js](https://www.npmjs.com/package/anvaad-js)). iGurbani and SearchGurbani still serve the **legacy STTM** scheme: `th` for dental ਤ, `tt`/`rr` for ਟ/ੜ, `ae` for ੇ, and glide-prefixed initial vowels, as in `koorrai thuttai`, `jae`, `outharee` ([iGurbani shabad 1](https://www.igurbani.com/shabad/1)). Shabad OS uses a third scheme, `toEnglish`: `dt` for ਢ, `tth` for ਠ, a single `|` for ॥, and dropped addak ([gurmukhi-utils](https://www.npmjs.com/package/gurmukhi-utils)).

These code-generated schemes are internally consistent but drift between versions. BaniDB went from `sathnaam … jae … koorrai` to `sat naam … je … kooRai`, and the anvaad-js README still shows an even older form with `mayray` and `kay` ([anvaad-js README](https://www.npmjs.com/package/anvaad-js)). ੴ alone appears five ways across sources: `ikOankaar`, `ikoankaar`, `Ik Oankaar`, `ik oankaar` and `Ek Ong Kaar`. Devotional 3HO usage adds Title Case, `Such` for ਸਚੁ and medial schwas dropped (`Kartaa Purakh, Nirbhao, Nirvair`), all **[snippet]** ([sikhdharma.org](https://www.sikhdharma.org/?p=3222); [Bandcamp](https://sirishabdsingh.bandcamp.com/track/aad-such-jugaad-such)). Diacritic schemes (ISO 15919, IAST, Shackle) appear almost only in print and scholarship. Online, losing diacritics is the norm, not the exception.

A detector also has to recognise one lookalike. **GurbaniAkhar/AnmolLipi font encoding** puts Gurmukhi into Latin letters (`siqnwmu krqw purKu … ]`). iGurbani's page source contains it, and it is the native input format of both anvaad-js and Shabad OS ([iGurbani](https://www.igurbani.com/shabad/1)). It is not a romanization, so it should be sent to the repo's existing `legacy.detect_encoding` and font converter.

| Family | Where found | Signature tokens | Lossy for | In repo? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BaniDB/STTM current | BaniDB API, STTM, anvaad-js | mid-word `T Th R`, `(n)`, `ikOankaar`, `'` addak, `\|\|` | ਦ/ਧ=`dh`, ਡ/ਢ=`dd`, ਣ/ਨ, tippi/bindi, final ਿ/ੁ dropped | `sttm` (with discrepancies, see bugs) |

| Shabad OS `toEnglish` | Shabad OS presenter, gurmukhi-utils | `dt`, `tth`, single `\|`, `tith`, no parentheses | addak, tippi/bindi, ਣ/ਨ, final vowels | No |
| BaniDB IPA | BaniDB `ipa` field | capital `G`, `t͡ʃ`, `t̪`, `ɓ`, `ɹ`, lines ending in `.` | addak, ਖ਼→`kʰ` | `banidb_ipa` |
| Informal / 3HO | social media, kirtan titles | Title Case, `w` for ਵ, `Ong`, `Such`, single `a` for ਾ | almost everything | No |
| Academic (ISO, IAST, Shackle) | books, GTrans **[snippet]** | `ṭ ḍ ṇ ṛ ā ī ū ṃ ṁ` | little | `iso15919`, `iast`, `shackle` |
| GurbaniAkhar ASCII | page sources, app inputs | `w q ] µ` inside "words" | not a romanization | via `legacy.py` |

Several systems could not be verified against real sources. No online source shows a separate Dr. Sant Singh Khalsa transliteration with dotted retroflexes, as the repo's `dr_sant_singh` map has: the transliteration printed next to his translation on iGurbani is legacy STTM ([iGurbani](https://www.igurbani.com/shabad/1)). No verbatim samples were found for srigranth's "Roman notation", Macauliffe, Gopal Singh, learner books, or UN 1972/ALA-LC (Interscript maps exist **[snippet]**, [Interscript](https://interscript.org/maps/un-pan-Guru-Latn-1972)). These gaps belong in the existing map-audit issue #4.

The "artifact-free" column removes candidates that exist only because of two drops in `_core.py`. One is addak after a vowel sign (ਸਿੱਖ→`sikha` in all eleven SystemMap systems). The other is tippi or bindi after an independent vowel (ਅੰਗ→`aga`). Fixing those two behaviours cuts candidate counts **roughly 2–3× for every SystemMap system**, and is the largest single reversibility gain available in the repo ([_core.py](https://github.com/thehimmat/gurmukhi-transliterate/blob/70468d9/gurmukhi_transliterate/_core.py)). Short and long vowels survive in every map as long as the writer is consistent. The aa/a and ee/i inconsistency that #16 describes is a property of ad hoc text, not of any system the repo implements.

The table understates how ambiguous real STTM text is. Real BaniDB English drops short word-final ਿ and ੁ (`moorat`, `purakh`, `paal`) ([BaniDB](https://api.banidb.com/v2/shabads/1)), but no forward engine in the repo ever drops them. In SGGS, **15.2% of tokens end in aunkar and 16.9% in sihari** ([Shabad OS tarball](https://registry.npmjs.org/@shabados/database/-/database-5.0.0-next.0.tgz)). Roughly a third of real BaniDB-romanized Gurbani words therefore carry an ambiguity that the matrix never shows. The Gurbani lexicon is the one resource that restores it reliably.

The existing Shackle reverse confirms what does and does not carry over. Round-tripping Shackle output gets **70 of 71 running Japji words (98.6%)** right; the one miss is the hiatus in ਨਿਰਭਉ ([reverse.py](https://github.com/thehimmat/gurmukhi-transliterate/blob/70468d9/gurmukhi_transliterate/reverse.py)). On the 20-word supplement it gets only 12/20, and most of those failures trace back to the forward engine. The `Ambiguity` record, `candidate_spellings` and `CorpusMatcher` are not tied to any system, so they can be reused. Three parts have to be replaced: the greedy tokeniser (because `kh` = ਖ or ਕ੍ਹ, `ai` = ੈ or a+ਇ, and `tt` = ਟ or a doubled ਤ), the rule that always inserts an inherent `a` between consonants (which breaks under schwa deletion), and the 256-candidate cap ([matcher.py](https://github.com/thehimmat/gurmukhi-transliterate/blob/70468d9/gurmukhi_transliterate/matcher.py)).

## A gated likelihood detector separates English from romanized Gurmukhi

The literature on detecting romanized Indic text mixed with English points to a consistent design: character n-grams plus dictionary lookup, with neighbouring-word context added by a CRF or other sequence model. Reported Punjabi–English token accuracies are about **88% for dictionary plus n-grams on small data, 96.8–97% for a CRF, and 98% for XLM-R**, all **[snippet]** ([ACL 2020.icon-demos.12](https://aclanthology.org/2020.icon-demos.12.pdf); [SSRN 4473174](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID4473174_code5909045.pdf?abstractid=4473174&mirid=1)). Bengali/Hindi–English work reaches 95.76% **[snippet]** ([Barman et al. 2014](https://aclanthology.org/W14-3902/)). The most relevant recent result is Google's: a linear character-n-gram classifier trained only on *synthetic* romanizations with realistic spelling variation reached **85.4% F1**, beating IndicLID's neural model (74.7%) on 20 romanized Indic languages. These figures come from the abstract and were not checked against the paper's tables ([Benton et al., EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.117/)). A dependency-free library can therefore get close to the CRF tier with no ML framework, provided it implements the context step itself.

No off-the-shelf tool fits. Only IndicLID and GlotLID v3 have a `pan_Latn` label. GlotLID scores it F1 0.963, but on only 4,782 test sentences against about 41k for `hin_Latn`, and its `eng_Latn` precision is only 0.794, so it over-assigns English ([GlotLID](https://github.com/cisnlp/GlotLID/blob/main/languages-v3.md)). IndicLID reaches only 0.75 F1 across its romanized classes and needs a 1.4 GB ensemble ([IndicLID](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicLID)). Both are fastText- or Transformer-based. They can serve as offline teachers or labellers for evaluation data, not as shipped components. General tools fall apart on short input: lingua scores **74.2% on single words but 99.7% on sentences** ([lingua-py](https://github.com/pemistahl/lingua-py)), and the romanized benchmarks deliberately left out sentences under five words because they were dominated by names and loanwords ([Bhasha-Abhijnaanam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15814)).

The recommended detector is a pipeline of gates followed by a scored decision:

1. **Script gate.** Any Gurmukhi or Devanagari character means the input is not English.
2. **GurbaniAkhar gate.** `w`, `q`, `]` or mid-word vowel capitals in shapes English never produces (`siqnwmu`) send the input to the font converter.
3. **Diacritic and IPA gate.** ISO/IAST diacritics, or IPA symbols, are close to decisive for Indic-Latin.
4. **Per-token log-likelihood ratio.** Each token gets a score made of:
   - English-lexicon log-frequency,
   - a hit in a romanized-Gurbani lexicon generated from Shabad OS text through every scheme,
   - a character 1–5-gram Naive Bayes ratio,
   - Gurbani cues: final `-u`/`-i`, `aa`/`ee`/`oo`, and aspirate digraphs.
5. **Neutral class.** A gazetteer of Sikh names, banis, places and honorifics labels tokens neutral, following the FIRE and LinCE convention of tagging named entities separately ([LinCE](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.223/)). This stops "Guru Nanak Dev Ji" from scoring as English.
6. **Context smoothing.** A two-state Viterbi with a switch penalty does the job the CRF does in the literature.
7. **Line decision.** The line takes the confidence-weighted majority of its non-neutral tokens. It abstains, or inherits the label from neighbouring lines, when the margin is small or the span is one or two tokens.

The researcher estimates **over 97% line accuracy for lines of five or more words, and 85–90% for single-word headings without document context [estimate]**. These have to be measured on the project's own PDFs.

## System identification should return equivalence sets, not a winner

`compare.identify_system` scores each system by token coverage, not likelihood. On Japji it ranks the true system uniquely first for **33.1% of lines and 15.1% of words**, and ties at the top in 50% of lines and 68% of words. Ties are broken by registry order, which favours `dr_sant_singh`. ISO 15919 and practical are never identified (0/10 lines), because the index holds only 14 and 3 signature tokens for them. All of these figures are **[engine output]** ([compare.py](https://github.com/thehimmat/gurmukhi-transliterate/blob/70468d9/gurmukhi_transliterate/compare.py)). The scorer has no "not Gurmukhi" outcome, so `The quick brown fox…` scores 0.756 for Shackle. It also lowercases its input, which erases STTM's `T`/`Th`/`R` evidence. The existing tests only check that the right system lands somewhere in the top three or four ([tests/test_compare.py](https://github.com/thehimmat/gurmukhi-transliterate/blob/70468d9/tests/test_compare.py)). Even so, user story US-005 is marked delivered.

Some systems cannot be told apart at all on many inputs. On text without ē/ō, ṛ, nukta or nasals, ISO, IAST and Shackle produce identical strings: `ādi sacu jugādi sacu` is the output of both ISO and Shackle. Thind and GFS differ only in a handful of letters. The identifier should therefore return a ranked list of *equivalence classes* with calibrated probabilities. It should use case-sensitive per-system character n-gram models, trained on each scheme's output over the Shabad OS corpus, plus a background English model and an "informal/unknown" class. Cheap discriminating features should be wired in directly:

| Feature | Indicates |
|---|---|
| Line ends in `\|\|` | BaniDB or legacy STTM |
| Line ends in `\|` | Shabad OS |
| Line ends in `.` | BaniDB IPA |
| Line ends in `]` | GurbaniAkhar |
| Mid-word capitals, `(n)` | BaniDB |
| `th` used for ਤ, plus `ae` | legacy STTM |
| `dt`, `tth` | Shabad OS |
| Title Case, `w` for ਵ, dropped medial schwa | informal or devotional |

One line already makes a good test vector. ਕਿਵ ਸਚਿਆਰਾ ਹੋਈਐ ਕਿਵ ਕੂੜੈ ਤੁਟੈ ਪਾਲਿ ॥ reads `kooRai tuTai … ||` in BaniDB, `koorrai thuttai … ||` in legacy STTM, and `koorrai tuttai … |` in Shabad OS ([BaniDB](https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/2/G); [iGurbani](https://www.igurbani.com/shabad/1); [gurmukhi-utils](https://www.npmjs.com/package/gurmukhi-utils)).

Most importantly, reversal does not need a single confident answer. When the identifier returns a set, the reverse transliterator should treat the system as a hidden variable: generate candidates under each plausible system and let the lexicon choose. A wrong pick between ISO and Shackle then costs nothing.

## Inverted maps plus a Gurbani lexicon beat models for this library

Three ways to reverse lossy and ad hoc romanization were compared. Candidate generation plus corpus ranking is, in effect, a noisy-channel model with a hand-built, weighted channel. It is the right default for a zero-dependency library whose main domain has a nearly closed vocabulary.

| Option | Evidence | Cost | Fit for this repo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Candidates from inverted SystemMaps plus variant rules, ranked by `α·channel + (1−α)·log P_lex`** | Lexicon rescoring of top-4 candidates added **about 12%** on Dakshina **[snippet]** ([Aksharantar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03018)). Saini & Lehal's dictionary + rules + corpus pipeline for Shahmukhi→Gurmukhi reports 91.37% **[snippet]** ([paper](https://learnpunjabi.org/pdf/gslehal-pap24.pdf)) | pure Python; lexicon about 150–311 KB gzipped | **Recommended default** |
| Pair n-gram / joint-sequence WFST (true noisy channel) | Google's Dakshina baselines use pair 6-gram models **[snippet]** ([Kirov et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.cl-2.2.pdf)) | Pynini needs native wheels; Sequitur is GPL-2.0 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/sequitur-g2p/)); a pure-Python re-implementation is substantial | Later upgrade to the channel model, if the rules plateau |
| Neural (IndicXlit, LLMs) | IndicXlit Punjabi top-1 **47.24 on Dakshina, 40.27 on Aksharantar native words** ([IndicXlit](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicXlit)) | fairseq/PyTorch, a 127 MB model plus an 850 MB frequency zip, no release since 2022 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ai4bharat-transliteration/)) | Optional `[xlit]` extra only, as an extra source of candidates |

The channel should be built in three layers:

1. **Near-lossless systems.** For ISO, IPA, IAST, Shackle, Gursevak, Sacred Nitnem and Sant Singh, a lattice parser over the inverted map gives roughly one spelling per word (artifact-free geometric mean 1.0–1.4).
2. **Lossy systems.** For STTM, Thind, legacy STTM and GFS, the inverted map *is* the collision class: Thind `t`→[ਟ, ਤ] becomes `kind='retroflex_dental'`. Search should walk a trie of the lexicon while expanding the lattice, instead of enumerating everything and filtering, because counts reach 1,056.
3. **Ad hoc text.** Weighted variant rules cover:
   - vowel length (a/aa, i/ee, u/oo),
   - schwa re-insertion,
   - Gurbani final ੁ/ਿ,
   - nasals (ਂ/ੰ/ਨ/ਣ),
   - addak,
   - aspiration,
   - ਰ/ੜ, ਸ/ਸ਼ and w/v.

   Vowel errors are **45%** of IndicXlit's errors **[snippet]** ([Aksharantar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03018)), so vowel-variant expansion constrained by the lexicon targets the largest class of errors. The weights can be fitted on aligned pairs generated by a port of anvaad-js (MIT) and on Aksharantar's CC0 portion.

The lexicon must be domain-tagged. Only **14.0% of SGGS word types** occur in a modern Punjabi gloss of the same text. SGGS has **zero addak and zero nukta**, against 63,044 addak-bearing tokens in the modern gloss ([Shabad OS tarball](https://registry.npmjs.org/@shabados/database/-/database-5.0.0-next.0.tgz)). A modern list would push Gurbani candidates towards spellings that never occur in Gurbani. The plan is therefore a Gurbani list with counts per source (67,517 types; SGGS 29,504, matching SikhiWiki's published 29,445 ([SikhiWiki](https://www.sikhiwiki.org/index.php/Words_in_the_Guru_Granth_Sahib))), with a modern IndicCorp-v2 list as a penalised fallback. Bhai Nand Lal should be kept separate: it is largely Persian, and SGGS vocabulary covers only 34.4% of its tokens. The ranker must stay soft. A lookup-only system scored **0.000 top-1** on held-out vocabulary ([indic-suggest](https://github.com/AshayK003/indic-suggest)), so the best candidate from the channel has to survive when nothing in the lexicon matches. Normalisation needs care. Under NFC the six nukta letters *decompose*, because they are composition exclusions, while Shabad OS stores them precomposed. A lexicon built from raw text and queried with NFC strings would silently miss every nukta word ([codepoints.net U+0A36](https://codepoints.net/U+0A36)).

## Gurbani lines should snap to canonical verses first

When the input is a Gurbani line, matching it to the canonical verse is both more accurate and more respectful than rebuilding it word by word, because it returns the exact stored text. The existing APIs are not enough. BaniDB's romanized search types 4 and 7 take only the first letter of each word and run an unranked `LIKE` substring query, so "so purakh niranjan" returns **253 results ordered by ShabadID**, with an unrelated line on top. Its MeiliSearch "omni" mode missed a lightly misspelled query entirely ([searchOperators.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhalisFoundation/banidb-api/dev/api/lib/searchOperators.js); [shabads.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhalisFoundation/banidb-api/dev/api/controllers/shabads.js)). ੴ contributes no first letter, so a user who types "ik oankaar sat naam" gets zero hits.

A prototype built during this research on Shabad OS SGGS (60,555 lines) works as follows:

1. Collapse first letters into coarse classes (t = ਟ ਠ ਤ ਥ, n = ਙ ਞ ਣ ਨ, any vowel = V).
2. Retrieve the 300 lines that share the most class trigrams with the query.
3. Re-rank by normalised Levenshtein on class strings and on consonant skeletons, with semi-global alignment for partial lines.

The queries were 300 random SGGS lines romanized by the repo's `dr_sant_singh` engine, so the clean result is itself **[engine output]**. Results ([Shabad OS tarball](https://registry.npmjs.org/@shabados/database/-/database-5.0.0-next.0.tgz)):

| Condition | Top-1 | Top-5 |
|---|---|---|
| Clean | **97.7%** | 98.7% |
| 10% random character noise **[simulated]** | 87.7–89.0% | 89.3–89.7% |
| 20% random character noise **[simulated]** | 74.3–77.0% | 77.7–79.3% |
| First 60% of words, with semi-global alignment | 80.3% | 92.7% |
| First 60% of words, without semi-global alignment | 23.0% | 44.0% |

The prototype runs at **13–22 ms per query in pure Python**. Real OCR confusions and real user romanizations were not tested.

The pitfalls are concrete. **9.9% of SGGS lines repeat verbatim** elsewhere, and 8,657 lines share a first-letter class string with a different line. ਸੋ ਪੁਰਖੁ ਨਿਰੰਜਨੁ… appears at both ang 10 and ang 348, so the matcher has to return every location unless context from neighbouring lines settles it. Shabad OS stores vishraam marks inline (`;` `,` `.`), so raw substring search fails. BaniDB verses can span two half-lines plus ਰਹਾਉ. Headers such as ਰਾਗੁ ਆਸਾ ਮਹਲਾ ੪ match loosely and should be down-weighted.

**When to use verse matching.** Try it first whenever the detector flags Gurbani cues (`mahalaa`, `rahaau`, `||`, frequent final -u/-i) or the input has about four or more words. Accept the match when the score clears a threshold; in the prototype, true matches scored below about 0.1 and runners-up above about 0.5. For multi-line input, decode in order and prefer lines from the same shabad. Otherwise, fall back to word-by-word reversal. Single words, modern Punjabi and names always go through the reverse transliterator. A word-level alignment of the matched line can still report which input word maps to which canonical word, so uncertain words can be flagged.

## Recommended approach

The library should get a single `detect → identify → match-or-reverse` pipeline that stays pure Python by default:

1. **Fix the forward engines first.** The bugs listed below poison every fixture and every model generated from engine output.
2. **Build data at build time.** A script that is not shipped produces a domain-tagged Gurbani lexicon from a pinned Shabad OS release, with per-source counts and consistent NFC handling. An IndicCorp v2 modern list serves as a penalised fallback.
3. **Detect.** Run the gates in order (script, GurbaniAkhar, diacritics/IPA), then a per-token log-likelihood scorer with a neutral named-entity class, Viterbi smoothing and an abstain option.
4. **Identify.** Replace coverage counting with case-sensitive likelihood models that return equivalence sets, including an informal class and an English reject.
5. **Match or reverse.** For Gurbani-looking input of about four or more words, try the class-index verse matcher first and return canonical text, every location of repeated lines, and a word alignment. Otherwise, or below the match threshold, reverse with a lattice over the inverted SystemMaps for the plausible systems, plus weighted ad hoc variant rules, ranked by `α·channel + (1−α)·log P_lex`, with a soft fallback for words outside the lexicon.
6. **Keep neural models and BaniDB as opt-in extras.** A `[xlit]` extra can contribute candidates; online BaniDB re-checks require the user to accept BaniDB's terms.

This changes the phase order proposed in #16. Verse matching moves ahead of the full ad hoc reverse, because it delivers the largest accuracy gain for Gurbani at the lowest cost.

## Conclusion

The question in #16 sounds like one problem, "reverse any romanization", but it splits into three regimes with very different difficulty. Gurbani lines are a retrieval problem over about 60k canonical lines, and they reach high accuracy with simple string methods. Standard schemes are a parsing problem that the repo's own maps already solve once the engine stops dropping marks. Only ad hoc single words and modern Punjabi are true transliteration problems, and there even the best neural model gets fewer than half right at rank 1. The closed Gurbani vocabulary is the asset that tips every trade-off towards a small, rule-plus-lexicon library: about 300 KB of public-domain word counts does work that would otherwise take a gigabyte of model.

The less obvious lesson is about testing. The repo's forward engine is both the generator of every current test fixture and the source of most measured ambiguity. Real BaniDB text differs from it in ways that cut both ways: it carries the apostrophe addak, and it drops final vowels on roughly a third of SGGS tokens. Until the engine bugs are fixed and the evaluation runs on real romanized text, any accuracy figure for detection or reversal mostly measures the engine, not the method.
